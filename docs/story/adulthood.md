# Chapters 3 to 5: Young adulthood, adulthood and old age

**DRAFT for Drew's reactions.** Young adulthood is written as big days like the earlier chapters; adulthood and
old age are outlines for now, to be filled in once the earlier chapters are built and played. Cast: `cast.md`.

The thread through all three: **Gran's little shop in Old Harbour.** It can become your business, your
father's second chance, a fight with the big supermarket, or something you sell to walk a different road.
Running it, or a career, is where money, risk and people come together.

---

## Chapter 3: Young adulthood (ages 20 to 29)

About seven hours of play. Life opens up: your own place, work, love, and the consequences of everything before.

### 1. Age 20: Gran's shop
- A dusty little shop in Old Harbour, shutters down for twenty years, in your name. Old stock, Gran's sewing
  machine, a photo of her young behind the counter.
- **Choice:** open it yourself, give it to your father for his fresh start, sell it for a big sum of livs, or
  rent it out while you study or work. This is the fork between the business life and other lives
  (`gran-shop`). Every path stays open later, at a price.
- **Hook:** the stallholder next door stares at you like she's seen a ghost.

### 2. Age 21: "Something else"
- The stallholder is **Mara**, 26, and she has your laugh. Your father's daughter, from before he met your
  mother. The "something else" he warned you about. She grew up three districts away and never knew you existed.
- **Choice:** welcome her, keep your distance, or confront your father. A sister you never had, or a rival for
  a father you only just got back (`met-mara`).
- **Hook:** Mara says, "Our father owes my mother a lot of money. Did he tell you that?"

### 3. Age 22: Nico's day in court, or Nico's return
- Depending on the teens: Nico is on trial and you're called as a witness; or he's back from working abroad
  with money and a business idea; or he's your partner, and wants more.
- **Choice:** tell the truth, protect him, or walk away. One of the biggest friendships in the game is decided
  here (`nico-fate`).
- **Hook:** whatever you chose, someone saw.

### 4. Age 23: Daniel
- If you promised Mrs Pell, you find her son Daniel, now a tired man in another city. Bringing him back to
  Willow Lane in time is a race. She dies soon after, at home, with or without him.
- Her will: something for you, depending on everything you've done since you were 5. Maybe her house.
  (`found-daniel`, `pell-will`)
- **Hook:** in her house, a letter: "Your grandmother asked me to keep this until you were grown."

### 5. Age 24 to 25: Love, and a wedding
- A relationship that's become serious (Juno, the first love, or someone new). Moving in together, meeting the
  parents, a proposal (who asks, and how), and a wedding day where every person you've treated well or badly
  shows up, or doesn't. Or you choose not to marry, and that's a life too (`partner`, `wedding`).
- **Hook:** at the wedding, Corin gives a speech nobody asked him for, and ends it by asking for money.

### 6. Age 26: The loan
- Corin has secretly borrowed against your shop (or your father's), and the debt is due. You face losing it.
- **Choice:** a bank loan from First Urlivana Bank (with interest and risk), help from Juno or Mara, Nico's
  "friends", or letting the shop go. Money decisions with real weight (`the-loan`).
- **Hook:** Corin is gone. Disappeared, with a note that just says "Sorry. Again."

### 7. Age 27: DailyVa
- The supermarket chain that crushed Mr Ambrose now wants your shop's corner. They offer a lot of money, or a
  partnership, or they open right next door.
- **Choice:** sell, partner, or fight them with everything you've learned since you were 6 and heard about the
  tab (`dailyva`). Mr Ambrose, old now, comes to watch.
- **Hook:** someone inside DailyVa offers to help you, for reasons of their own.

### 8. Age 28: Your little sibling
- Now 21: depending on how you raised them, they're doing brilliantly, in serious trouble, or about to make
  your mistakes. They need you, or they want to be nothing like you.
- **Choice:** take them in, give them a job, let them learn the hard way, or step back (`sibling-path`).

### 9. Age 29: A child of your own
- If you have a partner and choose to: your first child. Mum, now a grandmother, holds the baby. The night
  feels like the prologue, but you're the one in the doorway now (`first-child`).
- **Hook (end of young adulthood):** your father asks to talk, alone. He's ill.

## Chapter 4: Adulthood (ages 30 to 59), outline
About eight hours, with longer time jumps. Big days to write later:
- **Your father's last year:** illness, a last trip to the prison gate and the harbour, and the question of
  whether he and Mum make peace before he dies.
- **Building or holding:** your shop becomes a chain, your career peaks, or something collapses. A flood in Old
  Harbour, a price shock, a fire, a business partner's betrayal (risk, insurance, starting again).
- **Your child's childhood:** first day at school, and one day your child finds a box of letters of their own.
  Do you repeat your mother's choice?
- **Mum grows old:** the nurse becomes the patient. Who cares for her?
- **Corin returns,** old and broke. Forgive him, or not.
- **Juno and Nico at fifty:** the friendships, rivalries and loves of your youth, decades on.
- **Mara and your sibling:** the family you didn't choose, and the one you made.

## Chapter 5: Old age (ages 60 to the end), outline
About three hours, long time jumps. Big days to write later:
- **Letting go:** handing over the shop or retiring; who you trust with what you built.
- **Grandchildren:** a little one on Willow Lane who reminds you of you.
- **Becoming Mrs Pell:** the lonely neighbour, the one who sees everything, and a child who might be kind to you.
- **Last reconciliations:** the people still not forgiven.
- **The end:** however it comes, your life story: who was there, what you left, how you'll be remembered.
